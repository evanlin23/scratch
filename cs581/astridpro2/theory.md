# ASTRID-Pro under gene duplication and loss: an exact additivity theorem

*CS581 project, round 2. Supersedes `../gdl/results/theory.md`. The earlier note claimed the limiting matrix is
"always a tree metric". That is wrong: when the consistency condition fails, the four-point condition fails too
(Section 4). The four-taxon inequality (★) of the earlier note is replaced by a local edge-length formula.
Numerical checks: `code/verify_theory.py`, `results/verify_theory*.jsonl`.*

Source question (course slide 35): *"Can we find a distance correction for GDL so that ASTRID and NJst are
statistically consistent under GDL models?"* This note gives a yes answer for the orthology-restricted distance.
The answer has three parts: an exact formula for its limit, a necessary and sufficient condition for that limit to
be a tree metric with the species-tree topology, and a correction that removes the condition. ASTRID-multi itself
is **not** settled here.

---

## 1. Model

**Species tree.** S is a rooted binary tree on species set 𝒳, with root ρ and positive branch lengths. For a
non-root node c, write p(c) for its parent, sib(c) for its sibling and e_c for the branch above c. For an internal
node v, write v₁ and v₂ for its children.

**GDL model.** This is the standard birth–death model of Arvestad et al. (2003) and Rasmussen & Kellis (2012) without
ILS.
- A gene family starts as one copy at ρ. A root branch of any length can be added without changing anything below.
- Along branch e_c, every copy independently duplicates and is lost by a time-inhomogeneous linear birth–death
  process with rates λ_c(t) and μ_c(t). Rates may differ between branches.
- At an internal species node v, every copy present splits into one copy entering e_{v₁} and one entering e_{v₂}.
  Such an event is a *speciation event of v*.
- Copies alive at a leaf X are the genes of species X.
- The gene-family tree is the genealogy of the surviving copies. Extinct lineages are pruned and the resulting
  degree-2 nodes suppressed. Each internal node is the image of either a duplication or a speciation event. It is
  tagged **D** or **S** accordingly, and an S node u carries the species node M(u) whose speciation created it.

All that is used below is the **branching property**: given the copies present at a time point, their descendant
processes are independent and identically distributed. Any Markov branching model with this property works,
including non-linear duplication kernels and branch-specific rates.

**Survival.** For a non-root node c, s(c) is the probability that a single copy entering e_c has at least one
descendant at the leaves. For c internal, let q_c = 1 − (1 − s(c₁))(1 − s(c₂)), the survival probability of one copy
at node c. Then

  s(c) = 1 − G_c(1 − q_c),  and q_c = 1 for a leaf c,

where G_c is the generating function of the number of copies at the bottom of e_c, started from one copy. For
constant rates λ ≠ μ on a branch of length t (Kendall 1948), with r = λ − μ:
- α = μ(e^{rt} − 1)/(λe^{rt} − μ) and β = λ(e^{rt} − 1)/(λe^{rt} − μ);
- G(z) = α + (1 − α)(1 − β) z/(1 − βz).

For λ = μ, α = β = λt/(1 + λt). If every rate is finite, s(c) ∈ (0, 1] for every c.

**Data.** m i.i.d. gene families. The ideal inputs are true gene-family trees with the true root and true D/S tags.
Section 6 drops this assumption.

## 2. The estimator (ASTRID-Pro)

For two genes a and b of different species A ≠ B in the same family:
- The pair is **orthologous** if its LCA in the gene tree is tagged S.
- d(a, b) is the number of S-tagged nodes on the gene-tree path between a and b, counting the LCA. The gene-tree
  root counts like any other node.

Per gene family g, δ̂_g(A, B) is the mean of d(a, b) over the orthologous pairs of species A and B, defined when at
least one such pair exists. The ASTRID-Pro matrix is

  D̂_m(A, B) = mean of δ̂_g(A, B) over the families g in which it is defined.

A species tree is then built from D̂_m by NJ or FastME. This is ASTRID's two-level averaging restricted to
orthologous pairs and speciation nodes. `code/apro.cpp` computes it in O(Σ_g n_g · k_g) time, with n_g leaves and
k_g species per family, via per-node species aggregates at each LCA, instead of O(Σ n_g²) over leaf pairs.

## 3. The limit of the distance

**Lemma 1 (conditional expectation).** Let 𝓕 be the σ-algebra generated by the family's history, excluding the
descendant processes of the daughter copies that a speciation event of an internal node v sends off the path
between A and B. Then for every orthologous pair (a, b) of species (A, B):

  E[d(a, b) | 𝓕] = δ(A, B) := 1 + Σ_{v ∈ I(A,B)} s(off_{AB}(v)),

where L = LCA_S(A, B), I(A,B) is the set of internal species nodes strictly inside the species path from A to B
other than L, and off_{AB}(v) is the child of v that is *not* on that path.

*Proof.*
1. With true tags, the LCA of an orthologous pair is an S node, and M(LCA) = L. So the LCA contributes exactly 1.
2. The lineage of a runs from that copy at L down to A. In the *unpruned* genealogy it crosses each species node
   v ∈ I(A,B) on the A side exactly once, at a speciation event that also creates one daughter copy entering
   e_{off(v)}. The same holds on the B side.
3. In the pruned gene tree this speciation event is a node on the a–L path iff the off-path daughter has a surviving
   descendant. Otherwise the event becomes a unifurcation and is suppressed. Duplication nodes on the path are not
   counted by d.
4. So d(a, b) = 1 + Σ_v 1[daughter at v survives].
5. By the branching property, the daughter's descendant process is independent of 𝓕. Each indicator therefore has
   conditional expectation s(off(v)).
6. The event "(a, b) exists and is orthologous" is 𝓕-measurable: whether a and b exist and where their lineages meet
   depends only on processes on the A and B sides. ∎

**Corollary 2 (per-gene mean).** The set of orthologous (A, B) pairs and its size N_g are 𝓕-measurable. Hence
E[δ̂_g(A, B) | 𝓕, N_g > 0] = δ(A, B). The families are i.i.d., P(N_g > 0) > 0 and 1 ≤ δ̂_g ≤ |S|, so by the strong
law of large numbers, **D̂_m(A, B) → δ(A, B) almost surely** as m → ∞.

*Remark (conditioning).* Dropping families whose total species count is below 2 introduces no bias, because a
family containing an (A, B) pair already has two species. Pipelines that drop families with fewer than 4 species
condition on off-path survival, so the limit is no longer exactly δ. The bias is in the direction of larger s.

**Theorem 3 (additive representation).** For every non-root node c of S, put ℓ(c) = s(sib(c)), and for every
internal node v put h(v) = 1 − s(v₁) − s(v₂). Let d(x) be the ℓ-length of the root-to-x path. Define φ(v) = d(v) −
h(v)/2 for internal v and φ(X) = d(X) for leaves X. Then

  δ(A, B) = d(A) + d(B) − 2 φ(LCA_S(A, B)).

So δ is the path metric of S with **signed** edge lengths ω(c) = φ(c) − φ(p(c)):

  internal c:  ω(c) = β(c) := ½ [ s(c₁) + s(c₂) + s(sib c) − s(c) ],
  leaf X:      ω(X) = ½ [ 1 + s(sib X) − s(X) ] ≥ 0.

Once S is unrooted, the two root edges merge into one. Its length is ω(ρ₁) + ω(ρ₂), and when both children of the
root are internal this equals ½[s(ρ₁₁) + s(ρ₁₂) + s(ρ₂₁) + s(ρ₂₂)] > 0.

*Proof.*
1. A speciation node v ∈ I(A,B) that the A-side path enters from child c contributes s(sib c) = ℓ(c).
2. So the sum in Lemma 1 equals the ℓ-length of the A–B path minus the two edges into L. Those two edges are the
   edges above L's children c_A and c_B, and they contribute ℓ(c_A) + ℓ(c_B) = s(c_B) + s(c_A).
3. Hence δ(A, B) = 1 + [d(A) + d(B) − 2d(L)] − s(L₁) − s(L₂) = d(A) + d(B) − 2d(L) + h(L).
4. The edge lengths follow by subtracting φ along edges. For internal c:
   ω(c) = ℓ(c) − h(c)/2 + h(p)/2 = s(sib c) − ½(1 − s(c₁) − s(c₂)) + ½(1 − s(c) − s(sib c)). ∎

## 4. Consistency: a necessary and sufficient local condition

Call an internal node c **interior** if p(c) ≠ ρ.

**Theorem 4.**
- **(a)** If β(c) > 0 for every interior internal node c, then δ is a tree metric whose unrooted topology is S, with
  strictly positive internal edges. ASTRID-Pro followed by NJ is then statistically consistent: P(NJ(D̂_m) = S) → 1.
  By Atteson's (1999) ℓ∞-radius, the same holds for any method that is exact and robust on additive matrices with
  positive internal edges, such as BME/FastME.
- **(b)** If β(c) < 0 for some interior c, then δ violates the four-point condition.
  - Take any a ∈ c₁, b ∈ c₂, x ∈ sib(c), and y outside the clade of p(c); such a y exists because p(c) ≠ ρ.
  - On {a, b, x, y}, the true split ab|xy has the strictly *largest* four-point sum, and the two wrong splits tie
    for the smallest.
  - Every distance method that resolves a quartet by the smallest four-point sum is therefore inconsistent on these
    four species. This includes NJ, BME, FastME and least squares on 4 taxa. On larger trees the quartet is still
    mis-ordered in the limit, and NJ and FastME returned the wrong tree in our checks.
- **(c)** Balanced quartets, and quartets whose internal path does not use an edge with β < 0, are always resolved
  correctly.

*Proof.*
1. By Theorem 3, the four-point sums of a quartet with induced topology ab|xy are Q for ab|xy and Q + 2P for each of
   the other two splits. Here P is the signed length of the path between the quartet's two internal vertices.
2. For the quartet in (b), that path is the single edge e_c, so P = β(c).
3. (a) follows from the classical four-point characterisation (Buneman 1974) and the consistency of NJ on additive
   metrics with positive internal edges (Atteson 1999), combined with Corollary 2.
4. (b) holds because Q > Q + 2β(c).
5. (c) holds because a path made only of edges with positive length has positive length. ∎

**Theorem 5 (sufficient condition: no supercritical interior branch).** Let m_c be the expected number of copies at
the bottom of e_c, started from one copy. Then

  s(c) ≤ m_c · q_c ≤ m_c (s(c₁) + s(c₂) − s(c₁)s(c₂)).

So β(c) > 0 whenever

  m_c · q_c < s(c₁) + s(c₂) + s(sib c).

In particular β(c) > ½[s(sib c) + s(c₁)s(c₂)] > 0 whenever m_c ≤ 1, which holds when λ_c(t) ≤ μ_c(t) along e_c.

**ASTRID-Pro with ideal rooting and tagging is therefore statistically consistent under GDL whenever no interior
branch is supercritical.** Leaf branches and the two root branches may have any rates.

*Proof.* The bottom copies of e_c number N with E N = m_c, and each survives independently with probability q_c. A
union bound gives P(some copy survives) ≤ m_c q_c. ∎

Special cases:
- **λ = μ everywhere** (the FastMulRFS default and the DISCO default): consistent.
- **No loss** (μ = 0, any λ): s ≡ 1, so β ≡ 1, and δ is the speciation-node count. This is consistent, *including
  supercritical rates*. Supercriticality hurts only together with loss below the branch.
- **Failure needs three things at once:** a high-survival interior branch (s(c) large, which needs m_c > 1), and
  low-survival children and sibling.

**Counterexample (exact).**
- Tree: S = (((A:1, B:1)x:1, C:1)y:1, D:3). Branch e_x has (λ, μ) = (3, 0). Branches A, B and C have (0, 3). All
  other branches have no events.
- Survivals: s(A) = s(B) = s(C) = e⁻³ = 0.0498. q_x = 1 − (1 − e⁻³)² = 0.0971. The geometric copy number on e_x gives
  s(x) = 0.6835.
- So β(x) = ½(3e⁻³ − 0.6835) = **−0.267**.
- Prediction: δ(C, D) = 1 + s(x) = 1.6835 and δ(A, D) = 1 + s(B) + s(C) = 1.0996. Simulated with 20,000 families
  (true tags): 1.6794 and 1.0965.
- ASTRID-Pro + FastME returns AC|BD or AD|BC (FN = 1). ASTRAL-Pro, ASTRID-multi and ASTRID-Pro-S (Section 5) return
  the true tree.
- A random 6-taxon caterpillar with mixed rates (`random2` in `results/verify_theory*.jsonl`) has β = −0.034 on one
  interior edge. That is a second, much milder case: the predicted matrix gives FN = 1. With 2,000 families ASTRID-Pro
  is still correct, because the margin is tiny, but at 20,000 families it converges to the wrong tree (FN = 1). With
  inferred tags, ASTRID-multi and ASTRID-Pro-S (oracle first pass), the tree is correct.

**How often does it happen?** (`code/beta_scan.py`, `results/beta_scan.json`; exact β, no sampling)
- *Benchmark settings.* For the constant SimPhy rates of the DISCO conditions (dup 1e-10 to 1e-9, loss/dup 0, 0.5,
  1) and the FastMulRFS conditions, the minimum interior β is computed on every replicate's true species tree. It is
  ≥ **0.46** in all of them, including the supercritical loss/dup = 0.5 conditions. **So the theorem certifies
  ASTRID-Pro's consistency (with ideal rooting and tagging) on every standard simulated GDL benchmark.** Failures need
  rate variation across branches.
- *Random rates.* Per-branch λ, μ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3} on random Yule trees with 4–8 taxa, 20,000 draws. Some
  interior β < 0 in **230 (1.15%)**, rising from 0.5% at 4 taxa to 1.6% at 8 taxa. All 230 have λ > μ on the
  offending branch, as Theorem 5 requires.

## 5. A correction that removes the condition: ASTRID-Pro-S

Section 3 says the bias comes from the factors s(off(v)). If node v, entered from child c, is counted with weight
1/s(sib c) instead of 1, the conditional expectation becomes

  δ_S(A, B) = 1 + |I(A, B)|,

the number of internal species nodes on the path, *with the topology of S and unit internal edge lengths, for any
rates*.

**Estimating s.**
- Given a rooted species tree S₀, reconcile each gene tree to S₀ by LCA mapping.
- For each non-root gene node x with parent u where M(x) ≠ M(u), the lineage entering u's clade from x's side
  crossed every species node w strictly between M(x) and M(u), and then M(u).
  - At each such w, the off-path daughter was lost. Record a failure for the child of w on the other side.
  - At M(u): if u is S-tagged, record a success for the child of M(u) on the other side; otherwise record a failure.
- With true tags and S₀ = S, each record is an independent Bernoulli(s(·)) draw by the branching property. The
  success fraction ŝ is therefore consistent.

**Proposition 6.** If S₀ = S, with S's rooted topology, and tags are true, then the reweighted ASTRID-Pro matrix
converges a.s. to δ_S, and NJ on it is consistent under *every* GDL model.

*Proof.* Lemma 1 with weights 1/s, plus ŝ → s (Slutsky). δ_S has unit positive edges. ∎

**Caveats.**
- The guarantee is conditional on a correct first pass. If S₀ is wrong, the weights are wrong.
- Iterating from ASTRID-Pro's own wrong tree can be a fixed point. The previous pilot saw this on the counterexample.
- In practice we take S₀ = ASTRID-Pro + FastME, rooted on the edge that minimises total duplications (`apro -M pros`).
- A two-stage version with any consistent first pass, for example ASTRAL-Pro, is consistent by Proposition 6, but
  then the cheapness argument is gone.

*Relation to Asteroid* (Morel et al. 2023). Asteroid corrects ASTRID for gene-independent *missing data* by
averaging over induced subtrees. Here the "missingness" is copy loss below an off-path node, which depends on the
gene tree. The correction is a survival reweighting of single nodes, not a per-gene taxon-coverage weighting.

## 6. What changes with estimated rooting and tagging

**Species-overlap tagging on the true topology.** A true duplication whose two child subtrees end up with disjoint
species sets after losses is a *hidden duplication*, and it gets tagged S.
- Pairs across it are then treated as orthologs, and the speciations below it can be counted twice.
- Lemma 1 no longer applies. The expected distance picks up a term proportional to the rate of hidden duplications.
- Empirically this can *help*: on the counterexample, inferred tags give the true tree (FN = 0 in every run), because
  hidden paralogs add path length to exactly the pairs that the supercritical branch made too short.
- We have no theorem for inferred tags.

**MinDup rooting.** With true topologies and no ILS, MinDup returns the true root whenever the root branch carries no
duplication that is hidden from the overlap test (Zhang et al. 2020 make a similar argument for ASTRAL-Pro). If the
root is wrong, the "orthologous" relation changes near the root. The theory does not bound the effect.

**Counting the root.** The theorem needs the true root to be counted, as an S node. Counting a *wrong* root adds 1 to
every pair across the wrong root split. That is a non-additive perturbation, so `astrid-pro-r0` (root not counted) is
reported as an ablation.

## 7. What changes with ILS (DLCoal)

Under DLCoal, a locus tree is generated by GDL and the gene tree by the multispecies coalescent inside it.
1. Lemma 1 fails. Coalescence below a speciation can be deep enough that a gene-tree node's species set no longer
   matches a species node. Overlap tagging then marks incomplete-lineage-sorting events as duplications, and an
   orthologous pair's LCA need not map to LCA_S(A, B).
2. **No GDL** (single-copy families, pure MSC): every node is tagged S, so ASTRID-Pro with the root *not* counted is
   exactly ASTRID/NJst, which is consistent under the MSC (Liu & Yu 2011; Allman, Degnan & Rhodes 2018). Counting an
   arbitrary root breaks this. MinDup ties are everywhere when there are no duplications, so the root-counting
   variant is not consistent in general under pure ILS. This is a real design choice. The benchmarks report both,
   and a safe rule is: count the root only when the MinDup root is unique.
3. **GDL + ILS:** we conjecture that a combined result needs both conditions, no supercritical interior branch and the
   usual NJst condition, and that ILS enters only through the coalescent-unit lengths of the S-to-S paths. This is
   unproven. The data (DISCO ILS conditions and the GDL + ILS true-gene-tree curve) are the only evidence offered.
