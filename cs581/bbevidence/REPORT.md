# What makes good GCM evidence? Backbone evidence for MAGUS's merge

Exploration pilot, CS581 (UIUC), Fall 2026. Merge-only experiments: MAGUS's own 25 L-INS-i subset alignments are
fixed, and only the evidence fed to the Graph Clustering Merger (GCM) changes. Code: `code/`; raw rows: `results/`;
all tables in this report are generated by `code/tables.py` (`results/tables.md`), `code/mechanism.py`
(`results/mechanism.md`) and `code/summarize.py`.

## TL;DR

1. **Mechanism.** On proteins, GCM's final error is set by the **precision of its evidence**. Across 182
   (variant, replicate) pairs, the change in the fraction of evidence residue pairs that are true homologies predicts
   the change in final SPFP (Spearman ρ = −0.66, p = 1e-24) and in final error (ρ = −0.54). The change in the
   amount of *true* evidence predicts SPFN (ρ = −0.49). Clustal Omega backbones help because they are **more
   precise on cross-subset pairs** (+0.5 to +7 points), even though their recall is lower. "Less over-alignment of
   gappy columns" is not the mechanism: masking gappy backbone columns does nothing or hurts.
2. **Where the gain really comes from.** On the same sequence sets, the pairs L-INS-i and Clustal agree on are
   76–98% correct. The pairs only L-INS-i makes are **4–29% correct on 7 of 8 BAliBASE sets**. The pairs only Clustal
   makes are just as bad (4–36%), but Clustal makes fewer of them. So the useful signal is **agreement between
   aligners (consensus)**, not Clustal itself. M-Coffee's observation applies at the level of single residue pairs.
3. **Better recipe on proteins: filter the evidence by consensus.**

   | recipe (protein, 8 BAliBASE sets) | mean Δ error | W/T/L | p |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Clustal backbones (the pilot's finding) | −1.13 | 6/0/2 | 0.11 |
   | L-INS-i ∩ Clustal (pair intersection, same 10 sets) | −2.02 | 7/0/1 | 0.039 |
   | **L-INS-i, columns masked by cross-backbone consistency (t = 0.7)**: MAFFT only, no new alignment | **−1.73** | **7/1/0** | 0.008 |
   | L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2 `--op 3`: MAFFT only, +2 s CPU per backbone | −1.83 | 7/1/0 | 0.016 |
   | **(L-INS-i + Clustal), consistency-masked (t = 0.5)**: the only recipe that wins on all 8 | **−1.86** | **8/0/0** | 0.008 |
   | (L-INS-i + Clustal), consistency-masked (t = 0.7): largest mean gain | −2.53 | 7/0/1 | 0.016 |

   The intersection and masking recipes also **fix the replicates where Clustal hurt**. On BBA0134, Clustal was +1.50;
   the consistency mask gives −1.50 and L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2 `--op 3` gives −1.26. On the hardest set, BBA0081
   (56% error), the gain is 5.5–7.6 points.
4. **Nucleotides are the opposite regime.** On RNASim, 16S.M and ROSE, L-INS-i's unconfirmed pairs are mostly
   *right* (58–74% correct), and any filtering costs SPFN. On high-divergence ROSE (1000M2, 1000S1), Clustal or
   FFT-NS-2 evidence is catastrophic: +13 to +42 points. The only safe recipe across both data types is the union
   (L-INS-i + Clustal, 20 backbones): protein −0.67 (7/0/1); nucleotide +0.21, −0.08, +0.55, −0.70.
5. **When does filtering help? A reference-free test.** Among the L-INS-i pairs that the second aligner does not
   confirm, count how often the *other* L-INS-i backbones containing both residues also align them ("support").
   Support ≤ 0.59 on all 7 sets where filtering helped, and ≥ 0.64 on all 4 where it hurt (BBA0117, RNASim, 16S.M,
   1000M2). The threshold was chosen on these same 11 replicates (in sample) and the margin is thin; it needs
   held-out confirmation.
6. **Verdict.** Yes, there is a 4-week project: "consensus-filtered evidence for GCM", with a mechanism, a cheap
   MAFFT-only recipe, and a regime test. See §7. Negative results: more Clustal backbones (20, 30) are *not* better
   than 10. FAMSA hurts (+1.8). `--op 3` and gap-fraction masks do not help. The exception among MAFFT's own knobs is
   G-INS-i with `--unalignlevel 0.8`: −2.61 on BBA0101 on 5 backbones, at about the CPU cost of L-INS-i. It is
   promising, but it was screened on only 1–2 sets (§5.4).

## 1. Question

MAGUS (Smirnov & Warnow 2021) merges 25 MAFFT L-INS-i subset alignments with GCM. GCM's evidence is 10 backbone
alignments of 200 sequences (8 random sequences per subset), each aligned with L-INS-i
(`mafft --localpair --maxiterate 1000 --ep 0.123`). Their residue pairs become weighted edges between subset
columns; then MCL clusters the graph and a trace makes the clusters into a valid alignment. The pilot
(`cs581/sota2026` §4.3) found that aligning only the backbones with Clustal Omega lowered MAGUS's error by 1.6–1.9
points on 5 BAliBASE sets, almost all through SPFP, yet the first confirmation (BBA0117) went the other way. This
pilot asks:

- What makes good GCM evidence?
- Why does Clustal help, and when does it hurt?
- Is there a cheaper, larger or more reliable recipe, ideally with MAFFT kept as a black box, and does it carry to
  nucleotides?

## 2. Prior art checked

- **M-Coffee** (Wallace, O'Sullivan, Higgins & Notredame 2006, NAR 34:1692). Combines the MSAs of several aligners
  into a T-Coffee consistency library and builds a consensus. It beat each component on BAliBASE, HOMSTRAD and
  PREFAB, but only modestly, and it helps when the sources make different errors.
  - Here the same principle acts on GCM's evidence: residue pairs confirmed by two aligners are kept and the rest
    dropped.
  - Unlike M-Coffee, the consensus is not the output. It only filters the evidence of a divide-and-conquer merger
    whose subset alignments stay fixed.
  - Our consistency mask (`cons`) is in spirit T-Coffee's column consistency score, used to *delete* evidence rather
    than to weight a progressive alignment.
- **Collins & Warnow 2018, "PASTA for proteins"** (Bioinformatics 34:3939). Showed that the choice of subset aligner
  and merger matters for PASTA on proteins (BAliBASE).
  - It changed the subset/merge aligners, not the merge evidence, and has no consensus filtering of evidence.
- **Katoh & Standley 2016** (Bioinformatics 32:1933), MAFFT's over-alignment control (`--unalignlevel`,
  `--leavegappyregion`). It reduces aligned pairs in unrelated regions using pair-specific scoring matrices.
  - In MAFFT 7.450 (MAGUS's bundled binary), `--unalignlevel` is accepted **only with `--globalpair`** (G-INS-i); with
    L-INS-i MAFFT exits with an error. So we tested it on G-INS-i (§5.4).
- **MAGUS paper.** Tuned the number and size of backbones (with random selection) and Slow-mode HMM extension. It did
  not vary the backbone aligner or filter the evidence.
- The earlier literature audit (`cs581/literature/novelty_audits.md`) found no work on choosing or filtering GCM
  evidence. I found nothing new that changes that, but I did not run a fresh systematic search beyond the three
  papers above.

## 3. Methods

**Data.** Protein: 8 BAliBASE sets as filtered by the MAGUS paper (`cs581/data/balibase_clean`), one MAGUS
replicate each:
- BBA0039, 0067, 0101, 0134, 0154 and 0190 come from cached MAGUS runs on the worker branches.
- BBA0117 and BBA0081 are fresh MAGUS draws made here with `gcmx.prep` and the paper's flags.

Nucleotide controls: RNASim 1000 R0, 16S.M R0, ROSE 1000M2 R0 and 1000S1 R0 (1000L1 R0 if it finished; see
`results/`).

**Control.** Every replicate's merge with MAGUS's own 10 L-INS-i backbones (`linsi`) reproduces MAGUS's cached
result:
- exactly on 7 replicates;
- to within 0.01 points on BBA0101 and BBA0134;
- 56.28 vs 56.19 on BBA0081, where the trace is non-deterministic on this very hard set.

**Merge.**

```
python3 -m gcmx.run_magus -np 4 -s SUBSETS -b EVIDENCE_DIR -o OUT --graphclustermethod mcl \
        --graphtracemethod minclusters --graphtraceoptimize false -f 4
```

The command is the same for every variant. Only the files in `EVIDENCE_DIR` change.

**Variant grammar** (`code/bbe.py`):

| syntax | meaning |
|---|---|
| `T` | MAGUS's 10 backbone sequence sets realigned by tool T |
| `T@sK` | 10 new random sets, 8 per subset |
| `T@nN` | N backbones |
| `A+B` | union of evidence files |
| `A&B` | pair intersection: per backbone, only the residue pairs both A and B align |
| `A~5` / `A^5` | first / last 5 backbones |
| `A|gapT` | mask columns with gap fraction > T |
| `A|consT` | mask columns whose residue pairs are re-aligned by the other backbones containing both residues in < T of cases |
| `A|agree-X-T` | mask columns that tool X's alignment of the same set confirms in < T of pairs |
| `T@h` | backbones 1–5 only, for cheap screening of slow MAFFT settings; compare with `linsi~5` |

Details of the operators:
- **Intersection.** The intersection of two alignments of the same sequences is itself a valid alignment: split each
  column of A by B's columns, ordered lexicographically. It is written as an ordinary FASTA backbone. Verified: its
  pair set equals the set intersection exactly.
- **Masking.** Masked columns become MAGUS insertion columns: letters lowercase *and* `-` → `.`.
  `backboneToAlignMap` skips both, while a `-` still advances its column index. Verified against a literal port of
  `backboneToAlignMap`: the masked map equals the unmasked map minus the masked columns. Lowercasing alone gives a
  different (corrupt) map.

**Tools.**
- MAFFT 7.450 bundled with MAGUS:
  - FFT-NS-2: `--retree 2 --maxiterate 0`
  - `--op 3`
  - G-INS-i `--unalignlevel 0.4/0.8`
- Clustal Omega 1.2.4 with `--threads 1`.
- FAMSA 2.x.
- MUSCLE 5 was dropped: `-super5` took 429 s for one 200-sequence protein backbone on 1 core (vs ~4 s for Clustal),
  and `-align` is slower still.

**Diagnostics** (`code/bbe.py diag`, `code/pairdiff.py`), all on **cross-subset residue pairs**: pairs of residues
from different subsets, the only pairs GCM can use to merge.
- Backbone precision and recall against the reference restricted to the backbone's sequences.
- GCM graph edges between subset columns, with their weights.
  - An edge is "true" if at least half of the residue pairs between the two full subset columns are homologous.
- How much true and false weight ends up merged in the final alignment.
- How often the backbones repeat the subset alignments' own false within-subset pairs.
- Pair-level comparison of two tools on the same sequence sets: shared, A-only and B-only pairs, and their
  precision.

**Statistics.** Paired by replicate (same subsets): Δ = variant − control in error points, where error =
(SPFN + SPFP)/2 from FastSP. W/T/L uses a 0.05 tie band; p is the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. MAGUS's draw-to-draw
noise is ~0.5 points, but every comparison here is on identical subsets, so that noise does not enter.

**Hardware and timing caveat.** 4 cores; up to 5 experiment lanes ran concurrently, so recorded wall times (`bb_wall`,
`merge_wall`) are inflated and only roughly comparable. The per-tool CPU cost of one backbone, measured in isolation
(single thread, CPU seconds), is in `results/cputime.tsv` and §6.

## 4. Mechanism (Experiment 1)

### 4.1 Evidence precision drives SPFP; true evidence drives SPFN

From `results/mechanism.md`: 182 (variant, replicate) pairs over 11 replicates, all unmasked variants. Each
predictor is the variant's value minus the control's.

| predictor | ρ with ΔSPFP | ρ with ΔSPFN | ρ with Δerror |
|---|---|---|---|
| Δ precision of evidence pairs (cross-subset) | **−0.66** (1e-24) | +0.20 | **−0.54** (3e-15) |
| Δ total false edge weight (%) | +0.53 | −0.44 | +0.31 |
| Δ total true edge weight (%) | +0.19 | **−0.49** (3e-12) | −0.02 |
| Δ share of false weight merged into the final alignment | −0.38 | +0.21 | −0.34 |

GCM behaves like a classifier that inherits its precision from the evidence's precision and its recall from the
amount of true evidence. Which of the two dominates the final error depends on the regime: SPFP on proteins, SPFN on
nucleotides. This one picture explains every sign flip below.

**Reading the table.**
- **Why Clustal helps.** Where it helps, its backbones are *more precise* on cross-subset pairs: +1.2 to +7.4
  points, e.g. BBA0081 26.3 → 33.7 and BBA0154 75.4 → 79.0. The pilot's remark that "the Clustal backbones are not
  more accurate themselves" holds for the backbones' overall SP accuracy, but not for precision on the pairs GCM
  uses.
- **Why Clustal hurts on BBA0134.** Its precision gain is tiny (+0.5). Its recall loss is large (−5.7). And its own
  unique pairs are only 9% correct.
- **Why Clustal hurts on BBA0117 and the nucleotides.** The L-INS-i-only pairs that Clustal drops are mostly *right*
  (58–74%), so dropping them costs SPFN. On 1000M2, Clustal's backbones are simply bad: 17% precision, 14% recall.
- **The three hypotheses from the brief:**
  - **"Fewer false cross-subset edges / higher evidence precision":** yes, the main mechanism (§4.1).
  - **"Less over-alignment of gappy regions":** no.
    - Masking L-INS-i columns with gap fraction > 0.5/0.7/0.9 gives +0.13/+0.12/+0.32 on proteins (n=6; 1–3 wins).
    - L-INS-i-only false pairs are not concentrated in gappy reference columns any more than Clustal-only ones are
      (41–52% vs 38–56%; `results/*.pairdiff.jsonl`).
    - The bad L-INS-i pairs are confident, consistent-looking errors in alignable-looking columns, not gap
      over-alignment.
  - **"Errors decorrelated from the L-INS-i subsets":** weakly.
    - L-INS-i backbones repeat the subsets' own false within-subset pairs more often than Clustal backbones on all
      11 sets (e.g. BBA0154 55.0% vs 46.9%), i.e. same-tool errors are correlated.
    - But this predicts ΔSPFN better than ΔSPFP (ρ = −0.51 vs +0.30) and does not separate helped from hurt
      datasets.
    - The useful decorrelation is *between two aligners on the same backbone*, which is exactly what the
      intersection exploits.
- **Survival of false edges.** With Clustal evidence a smaller share of the remaining false weight survives into the
  final alignment on 9 of 11 sets (`false_w_survival` in `results/*.diag.jsonl`; e.g. BBA0101 39% → 30%). MCL drops
  false edges more easily when they are less consistently reinforced across backbones.

### 4.3 What predicts whether filtering helps?

- **With the reference:** the precision of the L-INS-i-only pairs separates perfectly.
  - 4–29% on the 7 sets where Clustal/intersection/masking helped.
  - 58–74% on the 4 where it hurt.
- **Without the reference:** use support, the fraction of times the other L-INS-i backbones containing both residues
  re-align an L-INS-i-only pair.
  - 0.16–0.59 on helped sets; 0.64–0.83 on hurt sets.
  - It needs only MAGUS's own backbones plus one cheap second alignment (Clustal or FFT-NS-2) of the same sets.
- **Caveat:** the threshold (≈ 0.6) is in-sample on 11 replicates with a thin margin (0.59 vs 0.64), and the 0.59
  set (BBA0039) is one where every effect is tiny.
- Simple dataset features do not separate the regimes: pairwise identity, length CV, and the share of residues in
  sparse reference columns (`results/features.jsonl`). BBA0134 and BBA0067 look alike, yet Clustal helps one and
  hurts the other.

  - On ROSE, the fast aligners' backbones are very poor (1000M2 Clustal backbones: 17% cross-subset precision). The
    sota2026 pilot found the same collapse of progressive aligners at full scale.
  - Even L-INS-i's own self-consistency is low there: backbones disagree because the data are hard, not because
    they are wrong.
- **The union is neutral to slightly helpful** (−0.70 on RNASim). It is the only recipe in this pilot that is safe
  on every data type: protein 7/0/1; nucleotide 2 wins, 2 losses, all ≤ 0.55 points.

### 5.4 Can MAFFT match it with its own settings? (Experiment 3)

Screened on 5 backbones and compared with the paired `linsi~5`, because each L-INS-i/G-INS-i backbone costs minutes.

| variant | sets | Δ vs control | Δ vs `linsi~5` |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-INS-i `--op 3` | BBA0101, BBA0134 | +0.16, +1.05 | +0.66, +1.18 |
| FFT-NS-2 alone | BBA0101, BBA0134 | −1.44, +1.53 | −0.94, +1.66 |
| L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2 (5) | 6 sets | −1.40 mean | −1.32 mean |
| G-INS-i | BBA0101 | −1.12 | −0.62 |
| **G-INS-i `--unalignlevel 0.8`** | BBA0101 | **−2.61** | **−2.11** (SPFP −5.2, SPFN +1.0) |
| G-INS-i `--unalignlevel 0.4` | BBA0101 | −1.22 | −0.72 |
| L-INS-i, gap-fraction masks 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9 (10 bb) | 6 sets | +0.13 / +0.12 / +0.32 | |

- A higher gap-open penalty on L-INS-i hurts.
- Gappy-column masks do nothing.
- **MAFFT's over-alignment control works when it is on.** G-INS-i `--unalignlevel 0.8` lowers SPFP by 5.2 points
  on BBA0101, about as much as the consensus filters. G-INS-i costs about the same CPU per backbone as L-INS-i
  (276 vs 226 s), so swapping MAGUS's backbone command to it would be cost-neutral.
  - **Caveat:** this is one set and 5 backbones. BBA0134 was still running at writing time (see
    `results/tables.md`).
  - In MAFFT 7.450 the option cannot be combined with L-INS-i.
- What works from inside MAFFT is **consensus**: a second, different MAFFT strategy (FFT-NS-2) intersected with
  L-INS-i, or L-INS-i's own cross-backbone consistency. Changing L-INS-i's parameters does not.

## 6. Runtime

**Per-backbone CPU cost** (one 200-sequence backbone, single thread, isolated; `results/cputime.tsv`):

| tool | BBA0101 (protein, ~465 aa) | RNASim (RNA, ~1550 nt) |
|---|---|---|
| MAFFT L-INS-i (MAGUS's backbone command) | 226 s | 1133 s |
| MAFFT G-INS-i | 276 s | (not run) |
| Clustal Omega | 13.7 s | 77 s |
| MAFFT FFT-NS-2 | 1.8 s | 14.6 s |
| MAFFT FFT-NS-2 `--op 3` | 1.5 s | 14.1 s |
| FAMSA | 1.1 s | (not run) |

From the first, uncontended runs: Clustal aligned MAGUS's 10 protein backbones in 13–40 s wall (4 in parallel).
The pilot measured ~190 s per L-INS-i protein backbone on 1 core.

**What each recipe adds on top of MAGUS:**
- **Self-consistency mask** (`linsi|cons0.7`): no new alignments. One Python pass over the 10 backbones: 9–23 s on
  these data, single-threaded and unoptimized (`prep_wall` in `results/*.results.jsonl`).
- **L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2:** 10 FFT-NS-2 runs, about 1–2 s each on proteins, plus the intersection.
- **L-INS-i ∩ Clustal:** 10 Clustal runs, about 4 s CPU each on proteins; much slower on 1500-nt RNA (the RNASim
  Clustal set took 906 s wall under load).
- **Clustal ∩ FFT-NS-2:** replaces all L-INS-i backbone runs. It is the cheapest recipe overall (~10× less backbone
  CPU than MAGUS on proteins), protein only.
- The merge itself is *faster* with filtered evidence, because the graph has fewer edges.

## 7. Verdict

**Is there a 4-week project beyond "use Clustal backbones"?** Yes. The pilot points to a better-founded and larger
effect: **GCM is precision-limited on proteins, and consensus between aligners is a cheap, reference-free way to buy
evidence precision.** A natural 4-week plan:

1. **Week 1: confirm on more draws.** Replicate on all 8 BAliBASE sets × 3–5 MAGUS draws for the three recipes:
   - `linsi|cons0.7`
   - `linsi&fftns2-op3`
   - `(linsi+clustalo)|cons0.7`

   Report paired Δ and Wilcoxon over ≥ 24 pairs. The bbtool sessions' Clustal draws can be reused as the comparison
   arm.
2. **Week 2: regime detection.** Validate the reference-free support statistic (§4.3) on held-out data: other
   BAliBASE sets, more ROSE/RNASim/16S replicates, and ideally HomFam-like protein families. Turn it into an
   automatic switch: filter when support < τ, else plain MAGUS or the union. Choose τ by cross-validation.
3. **Week 3: soft instead of hard filtering.**
   - Soft weighting by duplication was weak here. `linsi + (linsi&clustalo)` gives confirmed pairs double weight:
     −0.24, 6/0/2.
   - So MCL/minclusters needs unconfirmed pairs actually *removed*, or a weight transform strong enough to matter:
     support^k, or a per-edge support threshold, tuned jointly with the MCL inflation.
   - Goal: a continuous knob between the protein regime (filter hard) and the nucleotide regime (keep everything).
   - Add G-INS-i `--unalignlevel` as a MAFFT-native comparison arm.
4. **Week 4: end-to-end runs and runtime.** Run MAGUS end to end with the chosen recipe (`--gcmx-backbonetool`-style
   hook), wall-clock on 4 cores, and a write-up.

**Best recipe today:**
- **Protein, MAFFT as a black box:** `linsi|cons0.7`, MAGUS's own L-INS-i backbones with low-consistency columns
  masked. −1.73 mean, 7/1/0, p = 0.008, zero extra alignment cost.
  - Alternatively L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2 `--op 3`: −1.83, 7/1/0, +1.5 s CPU per backbone.
  - G-INS-i `--unalignlevel 0.8` backbones are the MAFFT-native candidate to test next.
- **Protein, most reliable:** `(linsi+clustalo)|cons0.5`, −1.86, 8/0/0, p = 0.008, SPFN almost unchanged
  (+0.21). Adds 10 Clustal runs: ~14 s CPU each, about 6% of the L-INS-i backbone cost.
- **Protein, highest mean gain:** `(linsi+clustalo)|cons0.7`, −2.53 (7/0/1). Or `linsi&clustalo&fftns2`, −2.40.
- **Protein, cheapest:** Clustal ∩ FFT-NS-2 (−2.28, 7/0/1; 30 backbones −2.63, 5/0/0), with no L-INS-i backbones at
  all.
- **Nucleotides, or unknown data:** the union L-INS-i + Clustal (neutral-to-helpful everywhere tested). Or keep MAGUS
  as is. **Do not** filter evidence on ROSE-like data.

**Honest limitations.**
- **One MAGUS draw per protein set.** Only 8 protein sets; the regime threshold is fitted in sample.
- **Contended wall times.** Times other than `results/cputime.tsv` were measured with several lanes sharing 4 cores.
- **Uneven coverage.** Some variants ran on only 5–7 of the 8 protein sets, and the expensive G-INS-i screening on
  1–2 sets. `results/tables.md` gives the n of every row.
- **Masked-variant diagnostics.** Diagnostic rows for masked variants that were computed before the fix (commit
  "diagnostics skip masked residues") still count masked residues. `mechanism.py` excludes all masked variants for
  that reason; the merge results are unaffected.
- **Clustal hurt on BBA0117 in both the bbtool confirmation and here** (+0.76). Our filters only neutralize it
  (−0.10 to +1.0), they do not fix it.
