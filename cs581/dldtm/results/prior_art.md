## Notes

1. **Phyloformer is protein-only.** The released code one-hot encodes the 20-amino-acid alphabet plus X and gap. The MBE paper and PF2 mention nucleotides only as future work or in notation. A DNA project cannot use PF weights out of the box. It would need to retrain on nucleotide simulations (training used 6× A100 80 GB) or use protein data. No public nucleotide Phyloformer was found.
2. **Phyloformer has a hard memory ceiling.** It tops out around 180 sequences × 500 sites on 32 GB, and memory is quadratic in n. 1000 taxa is out of reach directly, so subset-and-merge is the only route. PF outputs a distance matrix, so it fits naturally as the subset-tree method (PF + FastME) or as the auxiliary matrix for NJMerge/TreeMerge. GTM needs a guide tree instead.
3. **"Phyloformer 2"** (arXiv 2510.12976) exists: evoPF encoder plus BayesNJ posterior. It reaches about 200-300 sequences on 16 GB, is protein-only in all experiments, and its code is an anonymized repo. Its conclusion explicitly suggests supertree or ensemble heuristics to build larger trees, as future work [V].
4. **NeuralNJ** is the strongest DNA candidate: GTR+I+G, a public checkpoint, and tested up to about 100 taxa. Memory limits 100 taxa to under 5k nt. It beat Phyloformer on most benchmarks. There is no LICENSE file in the repo.
5. **Fusang** is DNA, runs on CPU, has public weights (MIT), and handles 4-40 taxa with alignments under 10k nt. It is the most practical CPU-friendly DNA base method for small subsets.
6. **Key prior art for the second question: Fusang + NJMerge-2 (Wang et al. NAR 2023).** For over 40 taxa the authors combined Fusang with the disjoint tree merger NJMerge-2. They split a 100-leaf MSA into ten 10-leaf subsets. On simulated 100-leaf data, mean RF was "slightly worse" than IQ-TREE and RAxML. On a 100-taxon vertebrate chromosome-1 dataset it scored RF 66, against 90 for NJ; ML ran out of RAM [V]. This is the closest published "DL + DTM" pipeline. It went only to 100 taxa, used NJMerge-2 rather than GTM, and the details are in the supplement (Fig. S10, Table S11).
7. **Zaharias, Grosshauser & Warnow 2022** is directly on point and from the GTM group. "Re-evaluating deep neural networks for phylogeny estimation: The issue of taxon sampling." *J Comput Biol* 29(1):74-89, doi:10.1089/cmb.2021.0383 [V]. They ran the Suvorov and Zou DNN quartets with QMC amalgamation (Quartet Puzzling was worse) on 20-leaf trees. DNN+QMC was less accurate than MP, NJ, and ML under simple models. Their explanation is that two-stage local-then-amalgamate approaches lose the benefit of taxon sampling. They predict that DNNs on 10-leaf subsets plus supertree methods are "unlikely to be generally successful" [V]. A DL+GTM project should cite this and test the claim, since subsets now hold 50-200 taxa rather than 4.
8. **DL quartets + ASTRAL / wQFM**: IQ-NET (2025 preprint) feeds DL quartets to ASTRAL [V]. A BUET MSc thesis seminar (June 2026, supervisor Bayzid) turns transformer quartet probabilities into weighted quartets for wQFM on real phylogenomic datasets [V, seminar abstract only]. Zou et al. 2020 used quartet puzzling [V]. Quartformer appears to combine DL quartets as well [U].
9. **DeePhy (2025)** is CNN-based. It decomposes into triplet trees and amalgamates them, a divide-and-conquer of sorts but on triplets, not disjoint subsets [U, snippet only].
10. **C-DEPP** (Jiang, McDonald, Perry, Knight, Mirarab, *Bioinformatics* 2024, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae361) is divide-and-conquer for a DL method. It is an ensemble of DEPP models over subtrees, scaling placement to a 200k+ leaf reference tree. It is placement, not de novo estimation, but it is a useful precedent showing that D&C for data-hungry ML "needs nuance" [V].
11. **No paper found that combines Phyloformer, PF2, or NeuralNJ with GTM, NJMerge, TreeMerge, or any supertree method.** Searches covered "Phyloformer + GTM", "deep learning divide and conquer phylogeny", and "disjoint tree merger + neural network". No DL method was evaluated at 1000 taxa for de novo sequence-to-tree estimation. The largest de novo DL results found are about 200-300 (PF2), 180 (PF), and 100 (NeuralNJ; Fusang+NJMerge-2).
12. **The Bayesian or variational DL methods** (VBPI-GNN, ARTree, GeoPhy, PhyloGFN) are fit per dataset on the DS1-DS8 benchmarks (27-64 taxa, DNA). They are not amortized pretrained estimators, and several need MCMC-derived topology support. They are poor subset estimators for this project; cite them as context only.
13. **Phylo2Vec and PhyloVAE are tree representations, not sequence-to-tree estimators.** Exclude them from the method comparison.
14. **Practical choices for a DNA 1000-taxon DL+GTM experiment:** (a) NeuralNJ on subsets of ≤100 taxa, needing a GPU; (b) Fusang on subsets of ≤40 taxa, CPU-friendly, giving about 25+ subsets for 1000 taxa; (c) Phyloformer only if retrained for DNA or run on protein data. GTM also needs a starting tree, such as FastTree or NJ on all 1000 taxa, and a decomposition, typically centroid-edge decomposition of that tree.
15. **Unverified items worth a quick check:** NeuralNJ CPU inference; GeoPhy citation details; the Quartformer and DeePhy author lists and code (bioRxiv rate-limited me with 429s); IQ-NET code availability; the Zou PhyDL nucleotide model.
