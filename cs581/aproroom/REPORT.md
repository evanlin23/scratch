# Where is the headroom for GDL species-tree methods? (aproroom pilot)

*CS581 pilot, 2026-10-10/11. Branch `claude/cs581-aproroom`. Pre-registration: `PREREG.md`, written before any
result was seen. Code is in `code/`, raw per-run JSONL in `results/`, and every table is regenerated by
`code/analyze.py` → `results/tables.md`. Machine: 4 cores, 15 GB RAM. Accuracy runs were 1 thread each, with up to 4–5
jobs at once. Q3 timings ran one job at a time on an otherwise idle machine.*

## 0. Answer in one paragraph

**Almost all the remaining error is gene-tree estimation error (GTEE), not tagging and not the summary method.**
- On true gene trees every method is nearly perfect:
  - FN ≤ 0.012 at 100 genes;
  - 0.000 at 1000 genes on DISCO;
  - 0.006 at 1000 species.
- Replacing estimated root/tags with the true SimPhy tags changes nothing (−0.001 to +0.000).
- On estimated trees error rises 5–15×:
  - 0.04 from 100 bp, 0.10–0.13 from 25 bp at 100 taxa;
  - 0.07–0.10 at 1000 species.
- GTEE is **80–95 %** of the error.
- More genes do not remove it. With 10,000 estimated genes ASTRID-Pro stays at 0.026, the same as with 1,000, while true trees give 0.000. So GTEE acts as a **bias**, not as noise that averages out.

**Between the best methods the gaps are small:**
- The per-replicate oracle (best of ASTRID-Pro, ASTRID-DISCO, Asteroid, ASTRAL-Pro3, wQFM-GDL) beats the best single method by only **0.001 (true trees), 0.008 (100 bp) and 0.012 (25 bp)** FN.

**Where methods do separate:**
- (a) **Short sequences / high GTEE.** The quartet methods degrade faster: ASTRAL-Pro3 is +0.023 FN worse than ASTRID-Pro at 25 bp (44/4/12, p = 5e-6), and DISCO+ASTRAL is +0.038 worse.
- (b) **Very high duplication.**
  - ASTRID-Pro beats Asteroid by 0.011 (p = 0.006) and ASTRID-multi by 0.013 on DISCO gdl_1e-9/5e-10.
  - On gdl_1e-9_0 ASTRID-Pro has 0.058, against 0.077 for ASTRID-DISCO. ASTRAL-Pro3 and Asteroid fail or time out there.
- (c) **High ILS.** ASTRID-Pro is slightly *worse* than everyone (+0.005 to +0.007; vs ASTRAL-Pro3 1/3/6, p = 0.047).
- (d) **Few genes (100).** wQFM-GDL was best on gtrees_10000_l1: 0.038 vs 0.056, 4/4 replicates.

**ASTRAL-Pro3's extra error is an objective failure, not a search failure:**
- In **60/60** inputs the true species tree has a *lower* ASTRAL-Pro3 quartet score than ASTRAL-Pro3's output.
- A search seeded with the ASTRID-Pro tree returns ASTRAL-Pro3's tree (diff +0.0000–0.0002).
- Reduced search (`-r 1 -s 0`) already reproduces default ASTRAL-Pro3 at 6–10× less time.

**Bottom line.** A new summary method that only changes the objective or the search can gain at most about
**0.01 FN** (about 1 of 97 edges) over the best existing method on these benchmarks. The large lever, up to
**0.03 (100 bp) – 0.10 (25 bp) FN**, is making the summary step robust to gene-tree estimation error. Examples are
support-aware weighting or contraction, using alignments or gene-tree uncertainty, or co-estimation. Robustness
to GTEE is also exactly where the methods already differ most.

**Pre-registered criterion: met, but the result is uninteresting.**
- The hybrid matches ASTRAL-Pro3 (+0.0002 at 100 bp, −0.0021 at 25 bp) at 5–10× less time.
- With 1000 genes, plain reduced search does the same; the guide only matters at 100 genes / 25 bp.
- The hybrid never reaches ASTRID-Pro's own accuracy.

**Why — search vs objective (`code/qscore.py`; FMRFS, reps 01–05, 60 inputs).** We scored the true species tree
under ASTRAL-Pro3's own objective (`-C`):
- The true tree scores **lower** than ASTRAL-Pro3's output in **60/60** inputs: 0.14 % lower at 100 bp, 0.66 % lower at 25 bp.
- It never scores higher, so there is no search failure.
- The ASTRID-Pro tree also scores lower than ASTRAL-Pro3's tree in 60/60 inputs, yet has fewer FN in 43 of them.

So better search for the ASTRAL-Pro objective cannot help. The objective's optimum is pulled away from the truth
by gene-tree error.

## 6. Where can a new method still beat the best existing ones, and by how much at most?

1. **Not by tagging/rooting.** True tags give ≤ 0.001 FN. 0 % of the headroom.
2. **Not by a better search for existing objectives.** 0/60 search failures for ASTRAL-Pro3.
3. **By a better objective on the same estimated gene trees: at most ≈ 0.008 (100 bp) – 0.012 (25 bp) FN.** That is
   the per-replicate oracle over 5 methods, ≈ 1 edge of 97, and it is optimistic because it picks the best method
   after the fact. In regime terms:
   - **high ILS:** about 0.005–0.007 gain is available over ASTRID-Pro; ASTRAL-Pro3 and ASTRID-DISCO are better there;
   - **missing data:** about 0.005, from Asteroid's correction;
   - **few genes:** ≈ 0.018 at 100 genes, where wQFM-GDL leads;
   - **very high duplication:** ASTRID-Pro is already best (0.058 vs ≥ 0.077 on gdl_1e-9_0).
4. **By robustness to GTEE: up to 0.03 (100 bp, 100 genes), 0.04 (DISCO 1000 genes), 0.09 (25 bp) and 0.09
   (1000 species) FN.** This is the L2 − L1 gap. It does not shrink with more genes (0.026 at both 1k and 10k).
   - Quartet methods lose more to GTEE than distance methods: ASTRAL-Pro3 GTEE 0.040/0.122 vs ASTRID-Pro 0.033/0.098.
   - Levers that could plausibly reach part of this gap:
     - gene-tree support weighting or low-support contraction (the parallel `wapro` session);
     - using several gene trees per locus (bootstrap/posterior) instead of one ML tree;
     - per-gene reliability weights (e.g. length or ASTRAL-Pro quartet-support outliers).

   A realistic target for a 4-week project is to recover 10–30 % of the GTEE gap. That would be −0.003 to −0.01 FN
   at 100 bp and −0.01 to −0.03 at 25 bp: a clear, significant win over all current methods, versus ≤ 0.01 for
   anything that treats gene trees as fixed.
5. **Speed is already decisive.** ASTRID-Pro is 0.2–20 s where ASTRAL-Pro3 takes minutes to hours and wQFM-GDL
   ~30 min at 1000 genes. Any GTEE-robust extension should stay in the distance framework to keep this.

## 7. Caveats

- **Timing.** Accuracy runs shared the machine (4–5 jobs on 4 cores). Only §4's scaling table is clean single-job timing.
- **wQFM-GDL coverage is partial.** FastMulRFS reps 01–05 (30 inputs per level), gtrees 50/100 genes, and one DISCO true-tree run. It was too slow for DISCO at 1000 genes (29 min per run).
- **ASTRAL-Pro3 on DISCO.** Reps 01–05 for 6 conditions, 01–02 for the others; the heaviest conditions time out. The DISCO estimated-tree runs of the fast methods were reused from the previous pilot (same software and inputs).
- **True tags** come from a locus-tree time rule. 0.05–0.2 % of nodes are ambiguous (time ties), more at 1000 species. With ILS between gene and locus tree, "true tag" is itself ill-defined (§2.1).
- **Scope.** The oracle bound covers these simulated benchmarks only (SimPhy, 100–1000 taxa). Empirical data with model misspecification may leave more room.
- **Asteroid.**
  - It crashed on the highest-duplication DISCO inputs (previous pilot).
  - In the previous pilot's scaling runs it failed on all taxon subsets because of a `[&R]` newick prefix (fixed here).
  - It was not run at 10,000 genes: about 1.4 GB at 1000 genes, growing with #genes.
