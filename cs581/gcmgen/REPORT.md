# Does consensus-filtered GCM evidence generalise beyond proteins?

Exploration pilot, CS581 (UIUC), Fall 2026. Merge-only experiments: MAGUS's own 25 L-INS-i subset alignments
and its 10 L-INS-i backbones are fixed; only the evidence given to the Graph Clustering Merger (GCM) changes,
and every variant is paired against MAGUS's own merge (`linsi`) on the same replicate. Code: `code/`; raw rows:
`results/*.results.jsonl`, `results/*.agree.jsonl`; all tables: `results/tables.md` (generated by
`code/summarize.py`, `code/select_recipe.py`, `code/switch.py`); train/held-out protocol: `SPLIT.md`.

## TL;DR

1. **A general recipe exists, and it is not a filter but two soft/structural changes to GCM's graph:**
   give the L-INS-i residue pairs that FFT-NS-2 does **not** confirm weight **0.03** instead of 1, and
   **delete GCM edges supported by fewer than 4 of the 10 backbones** (`wsoft0.03:linsi&fftns2#es4`).
   No reference, no data-type switch; extra cost = one FFT-NS-2 run per backbone (~1% of L-INS-i).

   | all replicates | n | mean Δ error | W/T/L | p | ΔSPFN / ΔSPFP |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | proteins (BAliBASE + AliSim) | 10 | **−2.76** | 9/1/0 | 0.002 | −3.34 / −2.18 |
   | DNA/RNA (ROSE, RNASim, 16S.M) | 14 | **+0.01** | 6/4/4 | 1.0 | +0.54 / −0.52 |
   | held-out only (chosen on the other 10): protein / DNA-RNA | 4 / 10 | **−4.17 / +0.06** | 4/0/0 / 4/3/3 | | |

   Held-out proteins: BAliBASE −1.22 / −1.98, AliSim −5.11 / −8.38. Worst DNA/RNA replicate +0.47 (RNASim_R1);
   the hard filter it replaces costs +15.2 on DNA/RNA (1/1/12).
2. **Why it generalises.** Soft weighting is self-calibrating: MCL normalises every column, so where the second
   aligner confirms almost nothing (ROSE, where FFT-NS-2's backbones are ~99% wrong) the graph is just MAGUS's
   graph scaled down, and where it confirms most pairs (proteins, 16S) the unconfirmed ones lose. Soft weighting
   alone fixes BAliBASE but *hurts* simulated proteins (+0.95 to +3.8); the edge-support cut fixes the simulated
   proteins (−3.3 to −8.3; low-support edges are 2–25% correct on every data type) and is neutral on
   DNA/RNA (`linsi#es3`: worst of 14 replicates +0.05). Together they win 9/10 protein replicates (1 tie).
3. **The "reliable second opinion" hypothesis is half right.** A second opinion that is as accurate as L-INS-i
   removes the DNA catastrophe — G-INS-i on ROSE 1000M2: hard intersection +0.87 instead of +32.5 — but still
   does not *help* on DNA: on these recall-limited nucleotide sets deleting even mostly-wrong evidence raises
   SPFN more than it lowers SPFP.
   L-INS-i with a random guide tree (GUIDANCE-style, 1c) is **not** reliable on ROSE (its backbones are ~99%
   wrong too; hard filter +4.1 / +16.7) and costs ~600× FFT-NS-2.
4. **Adaptive switch (4)** on measured FFT-NS-2 agreement (θ = 0.306, fixed on training) acts as a ROSE detector:
   held-out proteins −2.60 (4/0/0), DNA/RNA +0.11 (0/8/2, both RNASim replicates +0.55). It works, but the
   no-switch recipe above is better on the same held-out sets, and training never prefers switching to it.
5. **Safest single change:** `linsi#es3` (no second aligner): protein −2.03 (6/3/1), DNA/RNA −0.03 (3/10/1,
   worst +0.05), but its protein gain is almost all on the simulated sets (BAliBASE −0.36 to +0.13).
6. **Caveats:** most of the protein gain is on the simulated sets (−5.28 on 4); on the 6 BAliBASE sets the recipe
   gives −1.08, slightly less than the hard FFT-NS-2 filter (−1.26) that it makes DNA-safe. One MAGUS draw per
   dataset; only 10 protein replicates (6 BAliBASE, 4 AliSim) and 3 RNA;
   w and K were tuned on 10 training replicates; RNASim is the one data set where every evidence-removing recipe
   loses a little (+0.3 to +0.6).

## 1. Question and set-up

The bbevidence and protcons pilots found that keeping only the residue pairs that a second aligner confirms
(L-INS-i ∩ FFT-NS-2 `--op 3`) improves MAGUS on proteins (BAliBASE −1.83, held-out AliSim −2.27) but is
catastrophic on hard DNA (ROSE +7 to +42), because there the second aligner is near-random. This pilot asks for
a recipe that helps on proteins **and** does not hurt DNA/RNA, with no reference and no data-type switch.

**Data** (one MAGUS draw each; 25 subsets, 10 backbones × 200 sequences, MCL + minclusters, i.e. the paper's
flags):

| type | replicates | MAGUS error % | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| protein, BAliBASE RV100 (length-filtered) | BBA0039, 0067, 0101, 0134, 0154, 0190 | 4.7–29.2 | cached MAGUS runs (worker branches) |
| protein, AliSim LG+G4 + indels (1000 taxa, 300 aa) | SIMMOD_R1, SIMMOD_R2, SIMHIGH_R1, SIMHIGH_R2 | 11.2–24.0 | regenerated here exactly per `cs581/protbench` (same seeds); fresh MAGUS draws |
| DNA, ROSE 1000 | 1000L1, L2, L3, M2, M3, M4, S1, S2, S3 (R0), 1000M2_R1, 1000L1_R1 | 1.2–11.3 | cached MAGUS runs |
| RNA | RNASim 1000 R0 and R1, 16S.M R0 | 8.9–12.9 | cached MAGUS runs |

BBA0081 and BBA0117 were dropped: their fresh MAGUS draws would have cost ~1 h each on the loaded machine.
SIMHIGH_R2's MAGUS draw failed once (disk full) and was re-run successfully.

**Control.** `linsi` reproduces the cached MAGUS score exactly (e.g. BBA0101 29.19, identical to bbevidence's
control), and `dup2:linsi` (every backbone twice) is identical to `linsi` on all 6 sets tested, so GCM (MCL +
minclusters) is invariant to a global scaling of the edge weights.

**Statistics.** Δ = variant − MAGUS in error points, error = (SPFN + SPFP)/2 × 100 (FastSP). W/T/L with a 0.05 tie
band; p = two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank. Proteins and DNA/RNA are always reported separately.

**Train / held-out.** Fixed before any variant result was seen (`SPLIT.md`): training = BBA0101, 0134, 0067, 0039,
SIMMOD_R1, SIMHIGH_R1, 1000M2, 1000L1, 1000L2, 16S.M; held out = everything else. Both the adaptive-switch θ
(variant 4) and the final soft-weight/edge-support recipe were chosen on the training sets only; five extra DNA/RNA
held-out replicates (1000M4, 1000S3, 1000M2_R1, 1000L1_R1, RNASim_R1) were added after the selection and before
they were run.

## 2. Variants

All are merge-only and implemented in `code/gg.py` on top of `cs581/bbevidence/code/bbe.py`:

| name | meaning |
|---|---|
| `linsi&X` | hard intersection: per backbone, only the residue pairs that both L-INS-i and X align (bbevidence) |
| `wsoftW:linsi&X` | **soft weighting**: L-INS-i pairs that X confirms keep weight 1, unconfirmed pairs get weight W. MAGUS's edge weight is a residue-pair count summed over backbones, so this is implemented as each L-INS-i backbone at integer weight 1 plus `linsi&X` at weight 1/W − 1 (`code/run_wmerge.py`, a copy of MAGUS's `buildMatrix` loop with a per-file multiplier; checked equal to file duplication). `softW` = the same by duplicating files. |
| `linsi#esK` | **edge support**: delete every cross-subset GCM edge that fewer than K of the 10 L-INS-i backbones contribute to (protcons's `run_edgesup.py`) |
| `wsoftW:linsi&X#esK` | both: soft weights, then edge support counted over the 10 L-INS-i backbones only |
| `linsi\|cons0.7` | bbevidence's self-consistency column mask |
| second aligners X | `fftns2` (FFT-NS-2), `fftns2-op3`, `linsi-rt` (L-INS-i with shuffled input order **and** a random guide tree via `--treein`, i.e. GUIDANCE-style), `linsi-sh` (shuffled order only; dropped for cost), `ginsi`, `einsi` (`--genafpair --ep 0`), `linsi-op3`, `fftns2-nuc` (FFT-NS-2 with `--nuc` instead of `--anysymbol`) |
| `X@h`, `linsi~5` | backbones 1–5 only (cheap screen for the slow aligners), compared with `linsi~5` |

**What each variant family shows.**

- **(1) Reliable second opinions.** Whether the hard intersection is safe depends entirely on how good the second
  aligner's backbones are on the data, not on the data type:
  - FFT-NS-2 (and FFT-NS-2 `--op 3`, Clustal in bbevidence) backbones are **~99% wrong** on 9 of 11 ROSE sets
    (cross-subset pair precision 0.7–1.3%; FastSP on 1000M2 backbone 1: SPFN 0.99, SPFP 0.99 vs L-INS-i
    0.26 / 0.23). The exceptions are 1000M3 (18%) and the easiest set, 1000M4 (97%, MAGUS error 1.2%). Not an artefact of
    `--anysymbol`: FFT-NS-2 with `--nuc` gives the same alignments and the same +25.6 on 1000M2.
  - **L-INS-i with a random guide tree (1c) is just as broken on ROSE** (backbone SPFN/SPFP 0.99 / 0.99 on
    1000M2; agreement with MAGUS's L-INS-i 0.8%), so the GUIDANCE-style perturbation is not a "reliable second
    opinion" on hard DNA either: hard intersection +4.1 / +16.7. On proteins it is no better a filter than
    FFT-NS-2 (BBA0101 −1.37 vs −1.91; BBA0134 +0.08 vs −0.97), agrees with L-INS-i no more (overlap 0.61 / 0.63
    vs FFT-NS-2 0.61 / 0.69), and costs ~600× more. The shuffled-order-only variant (`linsi-sh`) was dropped for cost
    before it ran.
  - **G-INS-i (1a) is reliable on ROSE**: on 1000M2 its backbones are as accurate as L-INS-i's (SPFN/SPFP
    0.26 / 0.20; it confirms 75% of L-INS-i's pairs, FFT-NS-2 0.4%), and the hard intersection costs only
    +0.87 (vs +32.45 for FFT-NS-2 on the same 5 backbones); on BBA0101 it gives −0.89 hard / −1.21 soft.
    So the hypothesis is **half right**: a reliable second opinion removes the catastrophe, but on DNA the
    filter still does not *help* (+0.87 hard, +0.54 soft). Here the L-INS-i pairs G-INS-i rejects are mostly
    wrong (35% correct), and SPFP does fall (−1.9), but SPFN rises more (+3.7): MAGUS's error on these
    nucleotide sets is recall-limited, so deleting evidence costs more than it buys even when it is mostly
    junk. G-INS-i also costs as much as L-INS-i (~820–1,160 s per backbone here, under load), doubling
    MAGUS's backbone time.
  - E-INS-i (1b) and L-INS-i `--op 3` (1d) were screened on BBA0101 only (5 backbones, vs `linsi~5`): E-INS-i
    −1.41, `--op 3` −1.04; both confirm ~80% of L-INS-i's pairs (like G-INS-i) and reject pairs that are only
    12% correct, but they cost 565–1,400 s per backbone, and none was tested on DNA for lack of time. On the
    same 5 BBA0101 backbones the cheap FFT-NS-2 intersection is better than all three (−2.21; G-INS-i
    −0.89): a *less* similar second opinion removes more of L-INS-i's errors on proteins.
- **(2) Soft weighting** keeps DNA/RNA safe (w = 0.03: +0.03, 4/5/5; vs +15.2 for the hard filter) and keeps
  most of the BAliBASE gain (−0.06 to −1.89 on 6 sets), but on its own it **hurts the simulated proteins**
  (+0.95 to +3.8, 4/4). w = 0.5 and 0.25 change almost nothing anywhere; w = 0.01 starts to hurt ROSE (+0.96 on
  1000M2, +0.76 on 1000L3).
- **(3) Self-consistency.** The t = 0.7 column mask is not safe on ROSE (+7 to +17), where even L-INS-i's own
  backbones disagree. The **per-edge support threshold** is the opposite: it never hurts DNA/RNA
  (`linsi#es3`: 14 sets, worst +0.05) and gives the largest gains on the simulated proteins (−3.3 to −8.3),
  but helps BAliBASE only weakly (−0.36 to +0.13 on 6 sets).
- **Combination.** The two cheap, safe operations fix different problems (BAliBASE: precision of
  single-backbone evidence; simulations: junk low-support edges), so their combination wins on 9 of 10 protein
  replicates (one tie, BBA0039 at 4.7% error) and is neutral on DNA/RNA, with the two RNASim replicates as the
  only consistent losses (+0.34, +0.47).

## 4. Agreement statistics (reference-free) and the adaptive switch

Reference-free agreement = fraction of MAGUS's L-INS-i cross-subset residue pairs that FFT-NS-2's alignment of
the same backbone sequences also makes (mean over the 10 backbones; the per-backbone minimum is within 0.04 of the
mean everywhere). "prec unconf" uses the reference and is shown only to explain the effects.

| data | replicates | overlap with FFT-NS-2 | prec of FFT-NS-2's own pairs | prec of L-INS-i pairs FFT-NS-2 does not confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROSE (all but M3, M4) | 9 | **0.003–0.006** | 0.007–0.013 | 0.67–0.92 |
| ROSE 1000M3 / 1000M4 | 2 | 0.11 / 0.97 | 0.18 / 0.97 | 0.94 / 0.82 |
| RNASim R0 / R1 | 2 | 0.80 / 0.81 | 0.84 / 0.85 | **0.65 / 0.67** |
| 16S.M | 1 | 0.88 | 0.85 | 0.34 |
| BAliBASE | 6 | 0.61–0.96 | 0.67–0.94 | **0.24–0.41** |
| AliSim proteins | 4 | 0.66–0.86 | 0.69–0.89 | 0.49–0.57 |

Other second aligners (overlap / prec unconfirmed): random-tree L-INS-i BBA0101 0.61 / 0.33, BBA0134 0.63 / 0.37,
1000M2 0.008 / 0.77, 1000L1 0.009 / 0.77; G-INS-i (5 backbones) BBA0101 0.81 / 0.11, 1000M2 0.75 / 0.35;
E-INS-i BBA0101 0.79 / 0.13. Per-replicate rows: `results/tables.md`, "Agreement".

- The overlap separates "second aligner broken" (ROSE except 1000M4: ≤ 0.11) from "second aligner usable"
  (everything else, including the easy 1000M4: ≥ 0.61) with a wide gap, but it does **not** separate "filtering helps" from "filtering hurts": RNASim
  (0.80) and 16S.M (0.88) look like proteins, yet on RNASim the unconfirmed L-INS-i pairs are mostly right and
  every filter loses (+0.34 to +0.56), while 16S.M gains (−0.6 to −0.75). bbevidence's support statistic
  (`support unconf` in `results/tables.md`) separates them a little better (RNASim 0.76–0.78 vs 16S.M 0.70, BAliBASE
  0.40–0.62, SIM 0.53–0.73), but overlaps with the simulated proteins.

**Adaptive switch (variant 4), θ fixed on training** (`code/switch.py`; θ = the midpoint between training
overlaps that maximises the training mean):

| filter if overlap ≥ θ | else | θ | training (n = 10) | held-out protein | held-out DNA/RNA | held-out all |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `linsi&fftns2` | MAGUS | 0.306 | −0.73 (6/4/0, p = 0.016) | −2.60 (4/0/0) | +0.11 (0/8/2) | −0.67 (4/8/2) |
| `linsi&fftns2` | `wsoft0.03:linsi&fftns2#es4` | never filter hard | −1.14 | −4.17 (4/0/0) | +0.06 (4/3/3) | −1.15 |
| `linsi&fftns2#es2` | `linsi#es4` | 0.306 | −1.22 (8/1/1, p = 0.014) | (both arms run on too few held-out sets) | | |

- With θ = 0.306 the switch is in effect a "ROSE detector": it keeps MAGUS on 10 of 11 ROSE sets and filters
  everything else. Held out it loses only on the two RNASim replicates (+0.54, +0.55), filters the easy 1000M4
  harmlessly (−0.04), and gets −2.60 on the held-out proteins. It is a legitimate reference-free rule, but its training θ is not really
  determined (any θ between 0.005 and 0.61 is equivalent on training), and it adds nothing over the no-switch soft recipe.
- When the soft + edge-support recipe is allowed as the "else" branch, the training data choose **never** to
  switch to the hard filter, so the best switched recipe is just the no-switch recipe.

| 1000L1 | 2810573 | 0.84 | 0.168 | 0.01 / 0.04 / 0.08 / 0.92 |
| 1000L2 | 3352352 | 0.86 | 0.171 | 0.01 / 0.02 / 0.07 / 0.94 |
| 16S.M | 656588 | 0.45 | 0.028 | 0.12 / 0.13 / 0.22 / 0.87 |

- 45–86% of all GCM edges have support < 4, but they carry only 2–18% of the evidence weight, and only 1–25%
  of that weight is a true homology (vs 76–96% for support ≥ 4).
- Deleting them (`linsi#es4`) helps a lot on the simulated proteins (−4.1 to −8.3) and on most BAliBASE sets a
  little; on DNA/RNA it changes almost nothing (mean ΔSPFN +0.02, ΔSPFP −0.05; worst replicate +0.35 on
  1000M2). So the damage these edges do depends on the data. My reading (not tested directly): on ROSE their
  weight is too small next to the 9–10-backbone edges for MCL to act on them, while on the simulated proteins
  they connect clusters that should stay apart. The gain is almost all **SPFN** (ΔSPFN −4.8, ΔSPFP +0.2 over
  the 10 protein replicates), i.e. without the junk edges MCL/minclusters merges more true columns.
- This is the reference-free part of the recipe that needs no second aligner. It is close to the protcons
  edge-support baseline, but there K = 2–3 was only tested on proteins.

**(b) Single-backbone-precision errors that a second aligner sees.** The hard intersection with FFT-NS-2 removes
the L-INS-i pairs that FFT-NS-2 does not make. On proteins and 16S.M these unconfirmed pairs are 24–57% correct
(`prec unconf` in §4), the confirmed ones 86–97%, so filtering raises precision (ΔSPFP −2.6 on proteins). On
ROSE (except 1000M4) FFT-NS-2's own pairs are 1–18% correct and it confirms only 0.3–11% of L-INS-i's pairs, so
the intersection deletes ~90–99% of mostly-correct evidence (ΔSPFN +32 on average over DNA/RNA).

**Why soft weighting is safe where the hard filter is not.** With weight w for unconfirmed pairs, an edge that
competes with a confirmed edge is down-weighted by 1/w; an edge with no confirmed competitor keeps its *relative*
weight, because MCL normalises each column (MAGUS's GCM is invariant to a global scale, §1). On ROSE almost
nothing is confirmed, so the graph is MAGUS's graph scaled by w and the result is unchanged (Δ within ±0.25 on
11 of 14 DNA/RNA sets at w = 0.03; the other three are RNASim R0/R1, +0.42 / +0.56, and 16S.M, −0.64). On proteins most edges are confirmed and the unconfirmed ones lose. It is a
per-node, self-calibrating version of the switch in §4. The curve is steep: w = 0.5 and 0.25 do almost
nothing, w = 0.03–0.05 recovers most of the
hard-filter gain on BAliBASE, w = 0.01 starts to hurt ROSE (+0.96 on 1000M2).

**Why soft weighting alone fails on the simulated proteins.** On SIMMOD/SIMHIGH `wsoft0.03` *raises* error
(+0.95 to +3.8; ΔSPFN +2.4 to +9.1, ΔSPFP −0.5 to −1.4) although the hard intersection lowers it. The down-weighted edges are still in
the graph; there they act like the low-support edges in (a). Adding the edge-support cut removes that failure:
`wsoft0.03:linsi&fftns2#es4` gets −3.7 / −3.9 / −5.1 / −8.4 on the four simulated sets where `wsoft0.03` alone got
+1.1 / +3.8 / +1.3 / +0.95.

## 7. Verdict

**Best general recipe:** `wsoft0.03:linsi&fftns2#es4`: realign each of MAGUS's 10 backbone sequence sets with
MAFFT FFT-NS-2 (~2–15 s on proteins, ~15–80 s on 1,000-nt DNA), give L-INS-i residue pairs that FFT-NS-2 does
not make 1/33 of the weight, and drop GCM edges that fewer than 4 of the 10 L-INS-i backbones support.

**Honest verdict.**
- On proteins: −2.76 points on 10 replicates (9/1/0, p = 0.002); the held-out half (scored once, after the
  choice) is at least as good as training. The gain is uneven: on the 6 BAliBASE sets it is −1.08 on average,
  slightly *less* than bbevidence's hard filter on the same sets (`linsi&fftns2` −1.26), while on the 4
  simulated sets it is −5.28 (hard filter −2.39), where Clustal backbones hurt in protbench. So the recipe
  trades a little of the BAliBASE gain for DNA safety and gains a lot on simulated proteins.
- On DNA/RNA it is **neutral, not helpful**: +0.01 on 14 replicates, all within ±0.5, with small but consistent
  losses on RNASim (+0.34, +0.47) and on 1000M2 (+0.41), and small gains on 16S.M (−0.75) and some ROSE sets.
  "Does not hurt" holds to within half a point; "helps DNA/RNA" is not achieved by any variant here (the
  best DNA/RNA mean over ≥ 9 replicates is −0.06, `wsoft0.1:linsi&fftns2`, with 3/4/2).
- The original hypothesis ("filtering helps whenever the second opinion is as reliable as L-INS-i") is only
  half supported: reliability removes the catastrophe (G-INS-i on ROSE: +0.87 instead of +32.5) but the
  filter still loses on nucleotides, where MAGUS's error is recall-limited (deleting evidence raises SPFN by
  more than it lowers SPFP, even when the deleted pairs are 65% wrong). With FFT-NS-2 the unconfirmed L-INS-i
  pairs are 24–57% correct on proteins and 65–94% on ROSE/RNASim (34% on 16S.M).
- What carries the generality: the soft weighting deletes nothing (it only demotes unconfirmed pairs relative
  to confirmed competitors, so where nothing is confirmed nothing changes), and the edge-support cut removes
  only edges that at most 3 of the 10 backbones support, which are 1–25% true on every data type.

BAliBASE gains of 0.7–2 points are paired, but each comes from one decomposition); (2) implement both operations
inside MAGUS's own graph builder (one pass, no 20-file Python build) and report end-to-end time; (3) test
whether the edge-support cut should scale with the number of backbones (K/10 = 0.4) and whether the soft weight
can replace the second aligner with L-INS-i's own cross-backbone support (cheaper, and already neutral on DNA);
(4) understand RNASim, the one data set where every recipe loses a little.
